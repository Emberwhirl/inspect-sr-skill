# Domain 2: Inspecting conduct, governance and transparency

## What this domain asks

Check whether ethics, registration, recruitment, and resources make a real, prospectively planned trial plausible. Missed target sample size is **not** a trustworthiness marker. Outcome-reporting bias is **Risk of Bias**, not check 2.3.

| Check | Question |
|-------|----------|
| **2.1** | Are there concerns relating to ethical approval? |
| **2.2** | Are there concerns relating to the timing or absence of study registration? |
| **2.3** | Are there important inconsistencies between the publication and the registration documents? |
| **2.4** | Is the recruitment of participants implausible? |
| **2.5** | Are there concerns about the plausibility of conducting the study using the reported methods and resources? |

Each check **contributes to** the domain judgement; none (unlike typical 1.1) automatically ends the assessment.

---

## 2.1 Are there concerns relating to ethical approval?

**How**: Look in the publication(s) **and** the trial registration for a **reference number**, **panel/board**, and **date**. If a number is given, search it online to check it was not taken from an unrelated study. Ideally confirm the board exists and has authority — often difficult.

Reporting of ethics details became established only recently; **incomplete details in older studies may not indicate a problematic study**. Partial reporting can be **`Unclear`**.

**Avoid**: `Yes` solely because an old paper lacks an approval number; stopping after the manuscript when the registry has the number.

**Worked example**: “Approved by XXXX University Hospital Research Ethics Committee”; no number; registry silent; committee appears to exist; committee does not reply to contact → `Unclear`.

---

## 2.2 Are there concerns relating to the timing or absence of study registration?

**How**: Absent or **retrospective** registration makes it hard to know whether methods and results reflect a **planned** programme of work.

The check is about **timing**, not about the label “prospective.” Registration shortly after recruitment starts (only a small fraction of target N enrolled) might not strictly be prospective **and might not warrant concerns**. Judge timing against the details of the index study.

Registration became common only recently; rules differ internationally. **Nonetheless, with no registration there is no way to establish that the article describes a prospectively planned programme of work** (this still applies to older studies).

Use **Record History / Version 1**, not only the current page.

**Avoid**: Treating every non-prospective registration as automatic domain-serious; treating total absence as `Not applicable` (that answer belongs to **2.3**).

**Worked example**: ClinicalTrials.gov Version 1 submitted 6 July 2010; manuscript recruitment August 2008–April 2010 → retrospectively registered; cannot know whether sample size, outcomes, or eligibility were set in advance → `Yes`.

---

## 2.3 Are there important inconsistencies between the publication and the registration documents?

**How**: If the trial was registered (prospectively **or otherwise**), compare the paper to the **first version**, versions **before recruitment started**, and the **change history** — not only the latest record.

Unexplained discrepancies in **planned sample size, interventions, study dates, or eligibility** can be grounds for concern. Disagreements with registration documents **posted after the trial start** are particularly concerning.

**Failure to achieve planned sample size is not a marker of untrustworthiness** (many trials struggle to recruit; adequacy of N is out of scope).

This check is **not** outcome-reporting bias (that is RoB). Focus on other key aspects of the trial.

Major unexplained discrepancies with a **protocol**, if available, can also raise trustworthiness concerns.

**No registration → `Not applicable`.**

**Avoid**: Scoring under-enrolment as `Yes`; using 2.3 as an outcome-switching item; comparing only the live registry page (it may have been edited to match the paper).

**Worked example**: Retrospective IRCT record; control-group description differed from the paper and was amended to match **more than two years after publication**; paper recruitment April–September 2016 vs register April–May 2016 → `Yes`.

---

## 2.4 Is the recruitment of participants implausible?

**How**: Could a cohort of the **reported size** be enrolled in the **reported timeframe**? Do **not** confuse **full study duration** (includes follow-up) with the **recruitment period**. Publications often blur which was reported.

Needs **domain knowledge**: prevalence/incidence at that time and place; cases available at the site(s); staff enrolling; enrolment window (e.g. weekday daytime clinic).

Consider numbers **screened and consenting** — CONSORT is useful.

**Missed target N is not a trustworthiness marker.**

**Avoid**: `Yes` because they missed the power calculation; using total study length as the enrolment window.

**Worked examples**:
- Single-institution Poland catchment ~500,000; ruptured AAA; N=2,200 over 2 years; published incidence ~5.6–17.5 per 100,000 person-years → implausible → `Yes`.
- Recruited April 2018–September 2020 with complete 6-month follow-up, submitted January 2021 → calendar contradiction → `Yes`.

---

## 2.5 Are there concerns about the plausibility of conducting the study using the reported methods and resources?

Official wording (do not substitute a shorter paraphrase).

**How**: Could the protocol be **implemented as described** given setting and reported **resources (staffing, funding)**? Count **additional participant visits** versus usual care. Needs domain knowledge of time/cost to administer screening and outcomes.

**Avoid**: Scoring lean-but-feasible unfunded work as `Yes` merely because it is lean; scoring RoB quality (blinding, choice of control) here.

**Worked examples**:
- Single-centre moderate cognitive impairment; monthly MRI + bloods for 1 year; travel reimbursement; N=312 in 18 months; **no funding**; >3,700 visits plus unpaid MRI → `Yes`.
- PAD QoL (SF-36 and VascuQoL **in person**); **one** blinded researcher; **10** institutions; 6 months; supervised exercise not government-funded; no funding source → `Yes`.

---

## Reaching the domain judgement

No Yes-count rule. Recruitment and resource checks need content expertise. Combine 2.4 and 2.5 when both volume and delivery are impossible.
