# Domain 3: Inspecting text and figures

## What this domain asks

Look for duplicated or identity-incompatible text/tables, and for manipulated or duplicated figures. Ethical reuse of generic methods language is not a hit. Without plagiarism software and without anomalies, **`Unclear`** is allowed on 3.1.

| Check | Question |
|-------|----------|
| **3.1** | Are there concerns relating to duplicated content, such as text or tables, or text that is incompatible with the study? |
| **3.2** | Is there evidence of manipulation or duplication of figures? |

Both checks **contribute to** the domain judgement.

---

## 3.1 Are there concerns relating to duplicated content, such as text or tables, or text that is incompatible with the study?

**How**:
- Plagiarised text may be found with plagiarism software **or** noticed while examining multiple studies in the same review.
- Software used to **conceal** plagiarism (synonym spinning) may produce **tortured phrases** (guidance example: “counterfeit consciousness” for “artificial intelligence”). Unusual phrases are a **potential** marker. Researchers may **legitimately** use writing software when working in a language that is not their first language — do not treat language assistance as plagiarism by itself.
- Copied passages may **retain** the source study’s population or intervention, which then **does not match** the index study. That incompatibility is in-scope.
- Copying/editing **tables** from other papers is known in some problematic papers. **No software is currently recommended** for reliable detection. Reviewers may notice duplicate tables across studies included in the review.
- No plagiarism software **and** no anomalous text → may answer **`Unclear`**.
- Duplicated text is **not necessarily** problematic: generic methods descriptions, or authors recycling text **ethically**.

**Avoid**: `Yes` on any overlapping methods boilerplate; claiming a recommended table-duplication detector; requiring software (Unclear is allowed without it).

**Worked example**: Two laparoscopic-drilling trials from the same university, both eligible for the same review, share an **identical results table** → `Yes`.

---

## 3.2 Is there evidence of manipulation or duplication of figures?

**How**: Examine figures for improper manipulation or duplication. Known examples in the guidance: duplicated plots across panels of a multipanel figure; **manual addition of false error bars**; **shifting one survival curve** to create a second.

**Avoid**: Skipping figures because tables look consistent; calling ordinary reused software themes “duplication.”

**Worked example**: Six-panel bar chart, two groups, several timepoints; outcomes on **different scales** (days, mm, questionnaire scores); all six charts **identical** except axes and titles → `Yes`.

---

## Reaching the domain judgement

A clear identical table or cloned multi-panel figure is a strong `Yes` for the relevant check. Domain serious still requires the problem (alone or with others) to compromise trustworthiness — not a one-Yes algorithm (see SKILL.md).
